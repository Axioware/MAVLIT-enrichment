brand_signals.py - what's in here

Four functions so far, all write into brand_match_profile:

1. compute_creator_tier_profile
   Looks at how big the creators are that a brand has actually worked with.
   YouTube side = subscriber count on every sponsorship video for that brand.
   Instagram side = follower count of coauthor/tagged/mentioned users (NOT commenters,
   they're not creators the brand worked with).
   Averages both, buckets into nano/micro/macro/mega. Also saves the highest and
   lowest follower count seen on each platform (youtube_highest/lowest, insta_highest/lowest).

2. compute_audience_demographics
   This one's about the brand's actual audience, so no filtering - every instagram
   user linked to the brand counts (creators, tagged, mentions, commenters, all of it).
   Pulls gender split, top 5 countries, age group breakdown from the LLM-classified
   fields already on those users. Also saves sample_size so we know how much data
   the numbers are based on (need 20+ for it to mean anything).

3. compute_platform_presence
   Simple one - just checks brands_raw for which handles the brand actually has
   (instagram, youtube, facebook, tiktok, twitter) and saves True/False for each.
   tiktok/twitter booleans get saved but aren't used in scoring since V1 doesn't
   use those platforms.

4. compute_brand_embedding
   Builds a plain-English paragraph about the brand (name, niche, description,
   location, activity level, audience gender skew, dominant age group, top
   countries, creator tier, active platforms) then embeds it with
   sentence-transformers (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, 384-dim vector). Only mentions
   stuff that's actually known - if a signal hasn't been computed yet it just
   leaves that sentence out instead of guessing. Saves both the vector and
   the raw text (embedding_text) so you can see what got embedded.

All four are upserts - safe to re-run, just overwrites with fresh numbers.

There's also run_brand_signals() which just loops over brands scored >=50 in
initial_brand_score and runs all four functions on them (embedding runs last
since its text pulls from the other three).

Tested against brand 1417 (Zero Lifestyle), numbers came out sane.

Found and fixed a real bug along the way: sponsorship_activity_score had a
default of 0.0 on the model, and it turns out that default gets applied even
on the raw insert()-style upserts used here, not just normal ORM saves. So
every brand was silently getting a fake 0.0 instead of staying unknown, and
the embedding text was saying "limited activity" for brands where we
actually have no idea yet. Pulled the default, wiped the bad 0.0s that were
already saved.

Still missing: the actual sponsorship_activity_score computation (exists in
a different form in initial_brand_scoring.py, just not written into this
table yet).

apollo_contacts.py - the new Apollo piece

Separate file, new table (brand_contacts). Takes brands scored >=50, does
one FREE Apollo search (broad marketing-title sweep + brand's own location,
not a department filter - turns out Apollo doesn't really support filtering
search by department, only title/seniority), then asks Mistral to rank the
results and pick up to 5 best people for sponsorship outreach, then enriches
each of those 5 to reveal a real email. Only email for now - phone needs a
webhook setup Apollo requires that we don't have yet, so phone stays empty.

Every brand gets at least a marker row even if nothing's found, so it's
never re-queried - credits aren't free. Also updates brand_match_profile's
has_marketing_contact/contact_mode/best_contact_title_score based on
whichever contact ranked #1.

Went through two revisions:
1. After checking a reference script + real Apollo CSV output - switched
   from my own keyword-priority scoring to actually asking Mistral to pick
   the best candidate (falls back to keyword scoring if Mistral's not
   configured), and added the real fields Apollo returns
   (departments/subdepartments/functions) instead of guessing at a
   department filter that doesn't really work as an input.
2. User wanted options, not just one contact per brand - so now it stores
   up to 5 ranked people instead of 1, each with their own rank/email/title.
   Had to drop the old "one row per brand" unique constraint and replace it
   with a unique on (brand, person) pair instead, plus add a rank column.
   This does mean up to 5 Apollo credits spent per brand now instead of 1 -
   a deliberate tradeoff so creators have backup contacts if the top pick
   doesn't respond.

Tested for real against BetterHelp (twice, once per revision) - final run
got 5 verified emails: Emily McCoy, Justin L, Andrew S, Rania Benatia,
Justin Y - 4 of 5 are the exact same people that showed up in the
reference CSV. Also caught two real bugs along the way: Apollo deprecated
the old search endpoint (needed /api_search instead), and requesting phone
reveal without a webhook just breaks the whole call - both fixed.

Third revision (bigger candidate pool + fallback for outsourced marketing):
- Search now pulls 50 people instead of 25, and the title list got a lot
  bigger - added the 20 marketing-department categories the user gave me
  (Advertising, Brand Management, Content Marketing, Demand Generation,
  Digital Marketing, SEO/PPC, Social Media Marketing, etc.), on top of the
  sponsorship-specific terms (influencer, partnerships, growth) already
  there.
- Mistral now ranks ALL 50 candidates instead of just picking 5 - and if it
  ever forgets to rank someone in its response, the code adds them back at
  the end automatically so nobody found in search gets lost.
- All 50 ranked people get stored in the database now, not just 5 - it's a
  backup list for later. But only the top 5 still get the paid Apollo
  enrich call (real email) - the other 45 just have the free search preview
  data (title, obfuscated name, no email). Added an is_enriched column so
  it's obvious which is which. This doesn't cost any more Apollo credits
  than before - still exactly 5 enrich calls per brand max.
- New fallback: if the marketing search comes up completely empty, that
  usually means the brand outsources sponsorship stuff to an agency - so
  now it does a second free search for C-suite/founder/owner people instead
  and ranks those, so the creator still has someone to reach out to
  directly instead of nothing.

Tested against BetterHelp again after clearing the old 5-row test data -
got 50 stored, 5 enriched with real emails (same top pick as before, Emily
McCoy), 45 with obfuscated names and no email as expected. Full server
smoke test passed too. Didn't get to test the C-suite fallback against a
real brand yet since BetterHelp has an in-house team and never triggers
it - that path is verified by reading the code carefully, not a live test.

Phone numbers - got this working same day, but had to backtrack once:
- First try assumed Apollo pushes the phone number to a webhook URL once
  it's ready, so set up an ngrok tunnel + a receiver endpoint
  (/webhooks/apollo/phone) to catch it. Ran a real brand (Shopify) through
  the whole pipeline - got 5 enriched contacts with real emails, but no
  phone numbers ever showed up.
- Turned out my assumption was wrong. Made raw test calls straight to
  Apollo and actually looked at the FULL response instead of just grabbing
  the person field - Apollo doesn't push to the webhook at all in
  practice, it wants you to POLL a separate endpoint
  (GET /webhook_result/{request_id}) about every 10 seconds until it's
  ready. Confirmed this works for real - got back 3 real phone numbers
  this way. The webhook_url field still has to be present in the request
  (Apollo 400s without it) but it doesn't actually need to work - tested
  with a fake unreachable URL and polling still worked fine.
- Rewrote the enrich function to poll for the phone number instead of
  waiting on the webhook (up to ~70 seconds per person before giving up).
  Kept the webhook receiver in place just in case Apollo does push for
  some accounts, but it's not what the code actually relies on anymore.
- Important thing I found: phone reveal costs 8 Apollo credits per
  person, separate from whatever the email costs - so getting phones for
  all top 5 people at a brand is up to 40 extra credits, not free like I
  first assumed. Only happens if the ngrok webhook URL is configured in
  .env - unset it to skip phone lookup entirely and save those credits.
- Confirmed working for real: got actual phone numbers for 4 of the 5
  Shopify contacts (Deneil Mullings, Dayna Winter, Derek Gleason, Andrew
  Klegman). Left the last one without a phone since testing further
  wouldn't prove anything new.

Then simplified it further - asked if the webhook receiver (ngrok +
api/webhooks.py) was actually doing anything, and realized it wasn't:
since polling is what actually gets the phone number (not the webhook
push), Apollo never needs to reach back to us at all. Tested it with a
fake unreachable URL instead of the real ngrok one and it still worked
perfectly. So removed api/webhooks.py entirely and swapped the ngrok
URL/token setup for one simple on/off setting
(ENABLE_APOLLO_PHONE_REVEAL=true/false in .env). No more ngrok needed,
same phone numbers, less to maintain.
